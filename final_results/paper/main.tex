\title{Misfits: Text--Graph Disagreement for Detection and Re-homing in Social Networks, a Results Study}
\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Abhyudaya Vasisht}
\IEEEauthorblockA{[Affiliation]\\ abhyudaya2005@gmail.com}}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We study \emph{misfits}: accounts whose structural community disagrees with what their text says. The misfit score is one minus the probability, under a cross-fitted text-only classifier, of the node's own Leiden community. We report all results from one frozen result set of 22 experiments. Three are clean positives, six are partly supported, two mixed, seven not supported, one failed, and three descriptive. On synthetic graphs with planted mismatches, misfit detects them (AUC 0.73 to 0.999 over the grid) where degree and participation are at chance, and it beats a text-outlier baseline when community-blind text variation is present (0.904 vs 0.607 at $\sigma{=}1$, $\mu{=}0.5$). On TIMME, cross-community retweets land in the text-predicted community more often than chance even with bio-only text (0.599 vs 0.401). In a cascade simulation on a Pokec slice, rewiring misfits toward their text-predicted community raises correction reach by 10.92 points over no rewiring and 8.00 points over random rewiring; on TIMME the gain over random rewiring (+1.26 [$-$0.33, 2.22]) is not distinguishable from zero. Misfit-based blocking, misinformation detection and deception detection are not supported. Most positive results are synthetic and partly circular, and nothing was pre-registered.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
community detection, text--graph disagreement, link recommendation, misinformation, rewiring simulation
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
Social networks form communities by who follows or retweets whom, and users in a community usually talk alike. Some accounts do not: their profile text fits a different community than the one the graph places them in. Such nodes may be bridges, recruiters or infiltrators, or just users the follow graph under-serves. We call them \emph{misfits} and ask whether a simple, label-free score for them is useful for four jobs: finding bad actors, blocking misinformation spread, re-homing (recommending or rewiring links toward the text-predicted community), and detecting community-deception attacks \cite{kumari2021deception,fionda2018deception}.

This paper is results-first. Every number comes from a single frozen ground-truth result set, one row per number, with an interval where the source stored one. Our contributions are limited to what that set supports:
\begin{itemize}
\item A label-free misfit score and a leak control (bio-only text) for evaluating it.
\item Evidence that misfit finds planted mismatches on synthetic graphs, with conditions on text noise and nuisance text variation (E05).
\item Evidence on real TIMME data that engagement of misfits goes to the text-predicted community (E17), and simulation evidence that rewiring toward it increases correction reach on a Pokec slice (E21) and weakly on TIMME (E20).
\item An explicit list of what failed or is not supported, which is most of the original hypotheses (Section~\ref{sec:neg}).
\end{itemize}
The disagreement step follows the ambiguity idea of Sani et al.~\cite{sani2025aas}, applied to nodes rather than images.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
Let $G$ be a social graph and $t_i$ the community of node $i$ from Leiden \cite{traag2019leiden} (best of 100 runs). Let $x_i$ be the node's text embedding (sentence embeddings \cite{reimers2019sbert}, 384-d MiniLM). A text-only classifier is trained to predict $t$ from $x$. To keep a node's own label from informing its own score, it is \emph{cross-fitted}: each node is scored by a model trained on the other folds (5-fold, logistic regression). This gives $\pc[i,c]$, the probability that node $i$'s text belongs to community $c$. The misfit score is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{misfit}_i = 1 - \pc[i,\,t_i].
\end{equation}
A second score, \emph{disagreement}, is the Jensen--Shannon divergence between $\pc[i,\cdot]$ and a structural membership $p_T[i,\cdot]$ from label spread over Leiden runs.

\textbf{Leak control.} Real text may contain the community signal directly (for example a latest tweet about a party). We therefore report re-homing with bio-only text as the valid result and treat richer text (bio, latest tweet, GloVe) as a leak-control reference only.

\textbf{Re-homing target.} For node $i$ the target community is
\begin{equation}
\tau_i = \arg\max_c \pc[i,c].
\end{equation}
In E17 we ask whether held-out cross-community ties of misfits land in $\tau_i$, against a size-weighted chance rate.

\textbf{Rewiring intervention.} For each treated (misfit) node, \emph{rehome\_text} drops up to $k$ dormant own-community follow edges and adds the same number of edges into $\tau_i$ (degree-preserving). Controls are \emph{none}, \emph{random\_rewire} (same drop counts, random adds), \emph{rehome\_struct} (target is the structurally neighbouring community with most of the node's edges, in the style of Kumari et al.), and \emph{rehome\_text on random nodes}. We then simulate cascades with weighted-cascade acceptance (``wc'', $p{=}1/\text{in-degree}$) \cite{kempe2003influence}, with a content-gating strength $\beta$ (a node accepts content more readily from its own community by a factor $e^{\beta}$). Correction reach is the share of nodes reached by correcting content, in points.

\section{Experimental setup}
\textbf{Datasets.} \emph{TIMME} \cite{xiao2020timme}: 20{,}811 Twitter accounts with bios, tweets, and follow, retweet, like and mention layers. \emph{Pokec slice}: a regional slice of the SNAP Pokec network \cite{snap} with interest text and profile fields. \emph{Synthetic}: a degree-corrected stochastic block model ($n{=}4000$, $K{=}8$) with planted mismatched nodes; $\mu$ is community mixing, $\sigma$ text noise and ``nuisance'' the strength of a community-blind text block. The graphs have real community structure (E01): Louvain $Q$ is 0.256 (TIMME), 0.49 (Pokec slice) and 0.715 (full Pokec), against 0.035, 0.206 and 0.124 for a degree-preserving rewired null. Text predicts the Leiden community above the majority rate on TIMME (0.622 vs 0.318, E04) and barely on Pokec (0.278 vs 0.249), so Pokec misfit is weak.

\textbf{Baselines.} Centrality (degree, PageRank, betweenness); structural scores (participation, consensus entropy); a text score (content outlier: text far from the own-community centroid); Adamic--Adar and common neighbours for links \cite{adamic2003friends}; and controls (random, chance, none, random\_rewire, shuffled text).

\textbf{Metrics} (Table~\ref{tab:metrics}). Shares, AUC and precision are fractions; gains and reach are in percentage points (pp).

\textbf{Uncertainty.} Intervals are 95\% and of three kinds, always stated per table: paired bootstrap over simulations (E20, E21), bootstrap over users, and $t$-intervals over seeds (2--12 seeds). Where the source stored no spread (E05: mean of 3 seeds; E17: single run) we show none and bold nothing. We bold a best value only where an interval supports it.

\section{Main results (supported experiments)}
\subsection{E05: detecting planted misfits (synthetic)}
Fig.~\ref{fig:e05} and Table~\ref{tab:e05} compare misfit with five baselines over text noise $\sigma$ and nuisance, at $\mu{=}0.5$. Degree, participation and consensus entropy sit at chance (0.50--0.51) throughout. When text has no community-blind variation (nuisance 0), the text-outlier baseline is nearly as good as misfit (0.961 vs 0.964 at $\sigma{=}0.5$). The advantage appears with nuisance variation, as real text has: at nuisance 1.5 misfit reaches 0.904 at $\sigma{=}1$ against 0.607 for content outlier. Misfit and disagreement are within 0.025 of each other, and with three seeds and no stored spread we do not rank them. Verdict: supported, on synthetic data where the planted text mismatch is what misfit measures.

\subsection{E17: engagement goes to the text-predicted community (TIMME)}
For the top-10\% misfits of TIMME follow communities, Fig.~\ref{fig:e17} and Table~\ref{tab:e17} give the share of held-out cross-community ties that land in $\arg\max \pc$, against size-weighted chance. With bio-only text the share is 0.599 for retweets at $k{=}4$ communities (chance 0.401), 0.585 for likes and 0.508 for mentions; at $k{=}15$ it is 0.280 against 0.123 for retweets. Ties staying in the node's own community are 0.173 (retweets, $k{=}4$). The bio-only classifier predicts the community with accuracy 0.446 at $k{=}4$, so the result survives weak text. The leaky text variant gives higher shares (0.664 for retweets) and is kept only as a leak-control reference, not as a result. No interval was stored (single run); the tie counts (about $1.3\times10^5$) are not independent, so the gap above chance is large but not formally tested. Verdict: supported.

\subsection{E21: rewiring toward the text-predicted community (Pokec)}
Fig.~\ref{fig:e21} (right) and Table~\ref{tab:rew} show correction reach under the wc model with $k{=}5$ rewires per misfit. At $\beta{=}6$, \emph{rehome\_text} raises reach from 50.34 to 61.26, a gain of +10.92 pp [9.76, 12.13] over no rewiring and +8.00 pp [6.87, 9.10] over \emph{random\_rewire}. Rewiring toward the structural community gives only +2.46 pp, no better than random (+2.92), and applying \emph{rehome\_text} to random nodes gives +0.63 [0.05, 1.31]. The gain over random rewiring is clear from $\beta{=}1$ (+5.29 [3.98, 6.56]); at $\beta{=}0.3$ it vanishes (+0.39 [$-$0.88, 1.65]). Under the independent-cascade model (ic:2) the same contrast is +8.68 [6.96, 10.65] at $\beta{=}6$. Total infected share is nearly unchanged (+0.110 pp). Verdict: supported at $\beta\ge 3$, with the caveat that $\beta$ is swept rather than measured, selective exposure is assumed, and Pokec text is only weakly informative (E04), so the simulator rather than text accuracy drives much of this effect. Intervals exclude rewiring-draw variance.

\section{Further applications (partly supported)}
Table~\ref{tab:further} summarises the partly supported experiments; each carries an explicit caveat.

\textbf{E06, TIMME party validation.} Misfit scores 0.682 AUC (one-sided $p{=}0.019$) for party-label mismatches, against 0.541 for a shuffled-text null and 0.361 for degree. There are only 11 positives among 595 labelled nodes, and within degree strata participation (0.769) is above misfit (0.744).

\textbf{E08, regime boundary.} In a favourable synthetic cell (hub placement, $\beta{=}6$, mismatch share 10\%) misfit$\times$PageRank beats the best structural blocker by +1.38 pts [0.40, 2.36], while that blocker removes 33.0 pts. TIMME's implied gating is $\beta{=}1.44$, below the regime where misfit helps.

\textbf{E09, edge-level link friction.} On synthetic graphs at $\beta{=}4$, \emph{bridge\_struct\_x\_recv} removes 14.9 pts [13, 16.8] more cross-community infection than \emph{bridge\_struct}; at $\beta{=}0$ it is worse ($-$7.3 [$-$10.2, $-$4.4]) and on TIMME (40{,}000 links) it is not different ($-$0.5 [$-$1.2, 0.2]).

\textbf{E12, scenario map.} Detection precision@20 on planted mismatches is 0.787 [0.729, 0.846] for misfit, 0.6375 for disagreement and 0.0312 for PageRank (hub placement: 0.725 vs 0.369). For blocking, misfit helps only at strong gating ($+4.46$ [3.34, 5.58] at $\beta{=}6$) and hurts at realistic $\beta{=}1.4$ ($-2.15$ [$-2.59$, $-1.70$]).

\textbf{E13, infiltrator detection.} Misfit reaches 0.772 AUC [0.752, 0.792] against 0.508 for betweenness and 0.501 for PageRank and degree (text accuracy 0.61, random placement). It works for non-structural infiltrators when text accuracy is about 0.45 or higher; recruiters and hub or peripheral cases lose to PageRank or low-degree.

\textbf{E20, TIMME rewiring.} The headline gain over no rewiring is +4.72 pp [3.05, 6.91] at $\beta{=}6$, but the part attributable to text targeting, \emph{rehome\_text} minus \emph{random\_rewire}, is +1.26 [$-$0.33, 2.22], not distinguishable from generic rewiring. At $\beta{=}0.3$, near TIMME's measured agreement ($0.20$--$0.29$, E14), the effect is gone (+0.10 [$-$0.37, 0.56]).

\section{Limitations and negative results}
\label{sec:neg}
Most hypotheses were not supported (Table~\ref{tab:neg}). We separate ``failed'' (the pipeline was broken) from ``not supported'' (tested properly and rejected). The positive results are mostly synthetic and partly circular, since the planted mismatch is what misfit measures; real-data evidence is mostly negative or small. No experiment was pre-registered, each has many configurations (for example 6900 filter configurations in E11), and seeds range from 2 to 12. A count of intervals above 0 is not a corrected test: in E11, 764 of 6900 synthetic cross-community configurations are above 0, almost all at $\beta\ge 6$, while on real data 6 of 920 (TIMME) and 1 of 460 (Pokec) are, below what chance would give at a one-sided 2.5\% rate. The deception result (E22) comes with a circularity note: attack success is defined as a community-label flip, and misfit rises when the label changes, so the apparent detection of successful targets (0.640) is matched by a random-rewire control (0.663).

\section{Conclusion}
A label-free text-versus-graph disagreement score does three things the evidence supports. It detects planted mismatches on synthetic graphs when text carries community-blind variation. It identifies, on TIMME, the community that a misfit's cross-community engagement goes to, even with bio-only text. In simulation on a Pokec slice, rewiring toward that community increases correction reach well beyond random rewiring, if content gating is strong. On TIMME the same rewiring is not distinguishable from generic rewiring, blocking and misinformation detection are not supported, and deception detection fails a matched control. The honest summary is a narrow positive result on re-homing and detection, with strong dependence on synthetic design and assumed gating, and most original claims rejected.
